About Nakht

Historical Context

Nakht held the distinguished title of royal scribe and military commander during the Late Eighteenth Dynasty (~1390–1352 BCE), in Thebes, Egypt. This period corresponds to the reigns of Amenhotep II through Akhenaten, a time of significant religious and political transformation.

Titles: Royal Scribe (sš nswt) • Military Commander
Location: Thebes (modern Luxor)
Dynasty: Eighteenth (New Kingdom)

Dating and Provenance

Palaeographic analysis places this manuscript in the Late Eighteenth Dynasty (c. 1390–1352 BCE), based on:

  • Handwriting style consistent with Tell el-Amarna period manuscripts
  • Hieroglyphic forms matching contemporary Theban tombs
  • Ink composition analysis paralleling other 18th Dynasty papyri

Graphic Registers

The manuscript is organised into vertical registers (columns) with clear spacing between sections. Each register contains hieroglyphic signs arranged right-to-left, following standard Egyptian convention. Larger hieroglyphs are used for divine names, with standardised spacing for regular passages.
